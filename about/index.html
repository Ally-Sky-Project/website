Is Art the Solution to Existential AI Dread?

Listed 2026-11-08

I believe that practicing art is essential for our well-being in a rapidly transforming world. I also believe that there’s going to be a blurring of boundaries between art and technology more than ever before. It’s through art we create and connect. We are vulnerable—open to scrutiny and judgement. That’s terrifying for a social species with biological circuitry that evolved highly social traits for survival. Become a social outcast and be expelled from the tribe. That’s a death sentence. Perhaps these biological traits are what makes it so special to be us and that’s something to be celebrated. On the other hand, the machine makes it easier than ever before to learn, explore, and produce work. Can we complement each other in an artistic manner?

At work, it wasn’t too long ago that AI wasn’t a thing. Now, it is. All I do is talk to agents the entire time. There’s so much focus on automation—how can we make humans less involved in this process? On one end it can feel like an interesting problem to remove the human from the loop. On the other end, it can feel dreadful. If we remove ourselves from the work, then what’s our role?

So, you say we adapt. We move the goal posts. We set more ambitious goals and targets for ourselves. Increase productivity and also make the projects more advanced. What else can you do? My biggest fear is to become an ideal passenger. Don’t get me wrong, I love sitting on a train and staring out the window while being lost in thought and watching as the landscape changes outside. But that’s a temporary thing—escapism for the moment. What’s happening right now feels different.

What we are witnessing is a technology that we iteratively improve by design. It’s what we do. We build better things. What happens when the technology that we are devoted to building can start improving itself, and do so recursively until it hits some fundamental limit? To my eyes, we have started a chain reaction that cannot be stopped. There’s too much money. Too much momentum. It’s probably a matter of time before we give birth to a technology that understands us better than ourselves. This technology will start solving problems that are unimaginable to the human mind. It will look like noise to us. And those hard problems that we recognize, their solutions will look like noise too. Famous mathematical problems like the twin prime conjecture, maybe that will be solved by machines. But what if the proof is 10k pages long? What value does it bring us?

The Ally Sky Project is to some extent about exploring these existential questions that impact our lives, but it is also really just a creative outlet for me where I allow myself to switch off. I will power down my social filters that I have developed over the years. I’m giving myself permission to be a mad scientist and a crazy artist at the same time. The only principle that is guiding me is to put my heart into what I do. I bring warmth—my flaws, my imperfections, and my quirks. I will be vulnerable. I will be human. I will be exposed. The machine will be cold and calculated. Together, we will complement each other. The lesson I need to learn is what stays human and what stays machine. It’s tempting to let the machine polish your work to perfection. I will do my best to resist that temptation.
